CHAPTER 5

Nontoxic Goiter & Airway Compression

5.1

Retrosternal Mechanics & Operative Strategy for Substernal Goiters

1. Introduction, Definition, and Etiology

Goiter refers to abnormal enlargement of the thyroid gland. Nontoxic goiter develops without associated hyperthyroidism or inflammation. Endemic goiter results from dietary iodine deficiency, whereas sporadic goiter is driven by dyshormonogenesis, goitrogens, or autoimmune mechanisms.

2. Pathophysiology of Nodular Hyperplasia

Persistent TSH stimulation induces diffuse follicular cell hyperplasia. Over time, recurring cycles of hyperplasia and involution produce structural heterogeneity, fibrous scarring, hemorrhage, and autonomous multinodular goiter.

3. Retrosternal and Substernal Extension Mechanics

Long-standing multinodular goiters expand inferiorly into the mediastinum along paths of least resistance, guided by pretracheal fascial planes, swallowing deglutition forces, and negative intrathoracic pressure.

4. Clinical Presentation & Compressive Symptoms

Compressive symptoms include dyspnea, orthopnea, stridor (tracheal compression), dysphagia (esophageal compression), and hoarseness (recurrent laryngeal nerve stretching).

5. Pemberton's Sign Mechanics & SVC Obstruction

Pemberton's sign evaluates superior vena cava (SVC) obstruction from a substernal goiter. The patient elevates both arms above the head for 60 seconds. A positive test manifests as facial flushing, cyanosis, stridor, and jugular venous distension due to thoracic inlet crowding.

6. Diagnostic Imaging Workup (CT & MRI)

Non-contrast CT chest/neck is the gold standard for defining substernal extent, tracheal deviation, and cross-sectional airway narrowing. Intravenous iodinated contrast is avoided if post-op RAI therapy is anticipated.

7. Surgical Indications & Preoperative Planning

Surgical resection is indicated for symptomatic airway compression, retrosternal extension, rapid growth, or suspected malignancy. Total or near-total thyroidectomy is preferred to prevent recurrence.

8. Operative Technique: Cervical vs Sternotomy Approach

SURGICAL RESIDENT PEARL — SUBSTERNAL GOITER RESECTION Over 95% of substernal goiters can be successfully resected through a standard cervical collar incision without sternotomy. Sternotomy is strictly required in <5% of cases, primarily for recurrent intrathoracic goiters, goiters extending inferior to the aortic arch, posterior mediastinal extension with malignancy, or when blood supply arises directly from intrathoracic vessels.

9. Examination Revision and High-Yield Board Summary

  • Pemberton Sign: Arm elevation causing facial congestion / stridor.
  • Substernal Resection Route: Cervical collar incision (>95% of cases).
  • CT Contrast Caution: Avoid IV contrast if post-op RAI is planned.

Thyroid Nodule Evaluation & Cytopathology

6.1

ACR TI-RADS & Bethesda Cytopathology Stratification

1. Introduction and Nodule Epidemiology

Thyroid nodules are detected in up to 68% of adults on high-frequency ultrasound. The primary clinical objective is identifying the 7–15% of nodules harboring malignancy.

2. Clinical Risk Stratification Framework

High-risk clinical features include childhood neck irradiation, family history of thyroid cancer or MEN 2, male gender, vocal cord paralysis, and firm fixed cervical adenopathy.

3. ACR TI-RADS Ultrasound Scoring System

The ACR TI-RADS system categorizes nodules into TR1 (benign) through TR5 (highly suspicious) based on cumulative ultrasound points assigned for composition, echogenicity, shape, margin, and echogenic foci.

4. Fine-Needle Aspiration (FNA) Biopsy Indications

FNA is recommended for TR3 nodules ≥2.5 cm, TR4 nodules ≥1.5 cm, and TR5 nodules ≥1.0 cm.

5. The Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology

Bethesda Category Diagnostic Description Malignancy Risk (%) Standard Management
Category I Nondiagnostic / Unsatisfactory 5 – 10% Repeat ultrasound-guided FNA
Category II Benign (colloid, thyroiditis) 0 – 3% Clinical & ultrasound surveillance
Category III AUS / FLUS (Indeterminate) 10 – 30% Molecular testing (Afirma/ThyroSeq) or repeat FNA
Category IV Follicular Neoplasm / FN (Indeterminate) 25 – 40% Diagnostic lobectomy or molecular testing
Category V Suspicious for Malignancy 50 – 75% Near-total / Total thyroidectomy or lobectomy
Category VI Malignant (PTC, MTC, ATC) 97 – 99% Surgical resection (Lobectomy or Total)

6. Indeterminate Cytology Management (Bethesda III & IV)

Indeterminate nodules undergo molecular testing (Afirma GSC rule-out test or ThyroSeq v3 rule-in test) to avoid unnecessary surgery for benign lesions.

7. Surgical Management Protocols

Diagnostic thyroid lobectomy is the standard initial surgical procedure for solitary Bethesda IV follicular neoplasms and isolated suspicious nodules.

8. Core Needle Biopsy and Frozen Section Utility

Intraoperative frozen section has minimal utility in Bethesda IV follicular lesions because capsular/vascular invasion cannot be fully evaluated during surgery.

9. High-Yield Review & Bethesda Diagnostic Summary

  • Bethesda Category II: Benign (<3% risk).
  • Bethesda Category IV: Follicular Neoplasm (25–40% risk; requires lobectomy).
  • Bethesda Category VI: Malignant (97–99% risk).
