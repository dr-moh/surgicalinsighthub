PILLAR 1 Core Pathophysiology & Volume Dynamics
1.1

Body Water Partitioning & Gender/Age Variations

Total Body Water (TBW) represents the primary component of physical tissue mass, varying dynamically across the lifespan and between biological sexes:

  • Age & Sex Variations:
    • Neonates: 75% to 80% of body weight is TBW, which is extracellular-skewed, creating a high vulnerability to rapid dehydration.
    • Adult Males: ~60% of body weight is TBW, reflecting a higher muscle-to-fat ratio.
    • Adult Females: ~50% of body weight is TBW, driven by higher subcutaneous adipose tissue deposits (which are hydrophobic).
    • Elderly Adults: ~45% to 50% of body weight is TBW, indicating progressive sarcopenia and decreased muscle mass.
  • The Rule of Thirds Fluid Breakdown:

    TBW is structured into compartments using the following standard fractions:

    • Intracellular Fluid (ICF): Represents two-thirds (2/3) of TBW (~40% of total body weight), anchored by its dominant osmotically active cation, Potassium ($K^+$), and organic phosphate/protein anions.
    • Extracellular Fluid (ECF): Represents one-third (1/3) of TBW (~20% of total body weight), dominated by Sodium ($Na^+$) and balancing $Cl^-$ and $HCO_3^-$ ions. The ECF is further divided into:
      • Interstitial Fluid: Three-quarters (3/4) of the ECF compartment (~15% of body weight).
      • Intravascular Fluid (Plasma): One-quarter (1/4) of the ECF compartment (~5% of body weight).
1.2

The Three Postnatal Pediatric Fluid Stabilization Phases

Neonatal fluid therapy must adapt to the three distinct physiological phases of postnatal renal and volume development:

  1. Phase I (Early Postnatal / Extracellular Volume Contraction): Occurs during the first 1 to 3 days of life. There is a physiological diuresis of sodium and water driven by high levels of atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) and transitioning neonatal circulation. This leads to a normal 5% to 10% weight loss in term neonates. Over-resuscitation during this phase is linked to patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) and necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC).
  2. Phase II (Fluid Stabilization / Transition Phase): Occurs from day 4 to 7. Insensible losses stabilize, and renal tubular function matures, allowing the neonatal kidney to adjust to varying solute and free water ratios.
  3. Phase III (Growth / Positive Nitrogen Balance Phase): Occurs from day 8 onward. Characterized by true positive solute and nitrogen retention, enabling cellular division, skeletal growth, and linear physiological weight gain.

2.2

Paradoxical Aciduria in Contraction Alkalosis Mechanics

A classic ABSITE topic is the development of paradoxical aciduria in patients with severe volume depletion and chloride loss, such as in gastric outlet obstruction (e.g., pyloric stenosis, severe vomiting):

  1. Initial Insult: Repetitive loss of gastric hydrochloric acid (HCl) and water leads to **hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis**.
  2. ECF Depletion: Severe dehydration stimulates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS). Aldosterone acts on the principal cells of the cortical collecting duct to maximize sodium reabsorption, creating a negative luminal charge.
  3. Potassium Depletion: To conserve sodium, the kidney excretion of potassium via the ROMK channels increases. As potassium levels fall, a severe intracellular hypokalemia develops.
  4. The Aciduria Switch: With severe potassium depletion, the intercalated cells of the collecting duct are forced to swap potassium conservation for hydrogen excretion. The H+-ATPase pump runs at maximum capacity to reabsorb sodium and potassium, **excreting hydrogen ions into the urine despite systemic alkalosis**. This results in **paradoxical aciduria** (urine pH < 6.0).
  5. Correction: Infuse 0.9% Normal Saline with Potassium Chloride. Chloride delivery permits renal bicarbonate excretion, volume expansion deactivates the RAAS axis, and potassium replacement stops hydrogen ion wasting.
PILLAR 3 Calculations & Maintenance Formulations
3.1

Pediatric Holiday-Segar (4-2-1) & Adult Shortcut Math

Maintenance fluid rates prevent dehydration and support baseline metabolic processes:

  • Holiday-Segar 4-2-1 Pediatric Formula (Hourly Rate):
    • First 10 kg of body weight: 4 mL/kg/hour
    • Second 10 kg of body weight (11–20 kg): + 2 mL/kg/hour
    • Every 1 kg thereafter (> 20 kg): + 1 mL/kg/hour
    • Example for a 25 kg child: (10 × 4) + (10 × 2) + (5 × 1) = 65 mL/hour.
  • Adult Shortcut Equations:
    • Weight-Based Shortcut: **30–35 mL/kg/day** (corresponds to approximately 1.5–2.0 mL/kg/hour).
    • Standard Baseline: For a typical 70 kg adult, this yields 100–120 mL/hour of maintenance fluid. Normal adult maintenance requires ~1–2 mEq/kg/day of sodium and ~0.5–1 mEq/kg/day of potassium. For mature surgical candidates weighing > 20 kg, a simple bedside shortcut is: Patient's Weight (in kg) + 40 mL/hour.
3.2

Clinical Deficit & Third-Spacing Calculation Walkthrough

Case Study: 70kg Adult Patient Scheduled for Left Hemicolectomy

Clinical Scenario: A 70 kg male is NPO for 12 hours prior to a left hemicolectomy. Intraoperatively, he experiences moderate tissue exposure and third-spacing. Let's calculate his fluid requirement:

  1. Accrued NPO Deficit:
    • Baseline Maintenance: 70 kg × 1.5 mL/kg/hour = 105 mL/hour.
    • 12-Hour Deficit: 105 mL/hour × 12 hours = 1,260 mL.
    • Deficit Replacement Strategy: Replace 50% (630 mL) in the first hour of surgery, 25% (315 mL) in the second hour, and 25% (315 mL) in the third hour.
  2. Intraoperative Third-Spacing Loss:
    • Moderate abdominal surgery shifts fluids out of the ECF compartment at a rate of 4–6 mL/kg/hour.
    • Hourly Third-Space Loss: 70 kg × 6 mL/kg/hour = 420 mL/hour.
  3. Total Fluid Strategy for Hour 1 of Surgery:
    • Maintenance (105 mL) + 50% NPO Deficit (630 mL) + Third-Space Replacement (420 mL) = 1,155 mL of Lactated Ringer's in the first hour.
3.3

Landmark Glycemic & Crystalloid Trials

Decisions regarding fluid selection and metabolic management are guided by several multi-center clinical trials:

Trial Name Year Primary Intervention Clinical Outcome & Impact on Practice Guidelines
NICE-SUGAR 2009 Randomized 6,104 ICU patients to tight glycemic control (target blood glucose 81–108 mg/dL) vs. conventional glycemic control (target ≤ 180 mg/dL). **Tight glycemic control significantly increased 90-day mortality (27.5% vs. 24.9%)** and led to a high rate of severe, life-threatening hypoglycemia. Established the modern standard of targeting **140–180 mg/dL** in critically ill patients.
SMART 2018 Pragmatic trial randomizing 15,802 critically ill adults to balanced crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's or Plasma-Lyte 148) vs. 0.9% Normal Saline. **Balanced crystalloids significantly reduced the composite outcome of Major Adverse Kidney Events at 30 days (MAKE30)** (14.3% vs. 15.4%). Normal saline was associated with higher rates of new renal replacement therapy and persistent renal congestion.
SPLIT 2015 Double-blind crossover trial comparing Plasma-Lyte 148 vs. 0.9% Normal Saline in 2,278 ICU patients. No significant difference in the incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) or renal replacement therapy in a predominantly elective post-surgical population, suggesting that the choice of fluid has the greatest impact in high-volume resuscitation and septic cohorts (as validated in the later SMART trial).
PILLAR 4 Acid-Base Sequence & Electrolyte Management
4.1

The Delta-Delta / Anion Gap Mixed Acid-Base Algorithm

Evaluating complex mixed acid-base disorders requires a systematic, step-by-step mathematical approach:

  1. Step 1: Calculate the Serum Anion Gap (AG):
    AG = Na+ - (Cl- + HCO3-)

    • Correct for hypoalbuminemia: for every 1 g/dL drop in serum albumin below 4.0 g/dL, add 2.5 mEq/L to the calculated Anion Gap. Normal gap is **12 ± 2 mEq/L**.
  2. Step 2: Calculate the Delta Gap (ΔAG):
    ΔAG = Calculated Anion Gap - 12

    • This represents the excess unmeasured anions present in the ECF.
  3. Step 3: Calculate the Delta-Delta (Expected Bicarbonate):

    Add the excess Anion Gap to the measured bicarbonate to determine what the bicarbonate level would be if the metabolic acidosis were resolved:

    Expected HCO3- = Measured HCO3- + ΔAG

    • If Expected HCO3- > 26 mEq/L: A concurrent **Metabolic Alkalosis** is present (commonly due to concurrent vomiting or nasogastric suction).
    • If Expected HCO3- < 22 mEq/L: A concurrent **Non-Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (NAGMA)** is present (commonly due to normal saline administration or renal tubular acidosis).
4.2

Electrolyte Derangement Velocity Rules & Free Water Deficit

Rapid correction of sodium abnormalities can cause neurological injury. Correction rates must adhere to strict velocity limits:

  • Chronic Hyponatremia Correction Velocity:
    • Limit correction to ≤ 8–10 mEq/L in a 24-hour period (or ≤ 0.5 mEq/L/hour).
    • **Pathophysiology:** Rapid sodium replacement increases extracellular osmolarity, pulling water out of brain cells. In the pontine sheath, this causes cell shrinkage and **Central Pontine Myelinolysis (Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome)**, which presents with flaccid paralysis, dysarthria, and dysphagia.
  • Hypernatremia & Free Water Deficit (FWD) Math:
    • Calculate the free water deficit to guide hypotonic fluid (D5W) replacement:
    FWD (Liters) = Coeff × Total Body Weight × [ (Measured Na+ / 140) - 1 ]

    • Coefficients: 0.6 for adult males, 0.5 for adult females, 0.5 for elderly males, 0.45 for elderly females.
    • **Correction Velocity:** Correction must not exceed **10 mEq/L/day** (or 0.5 mEq/L/hour) to prevent astrocyte swelling and **cerebral edema**, which can cause seizures, herniation, and death.

VIVA SCENARIO — gastric outlet obstruction alkalosis
A 4-week-old male infant presents with non-bilious, projectile vomiting. His weight is 4.0 kg. The laboratory panel shows: Na+ 132 mEq/L, K+ 2.9 mEq/L, Cl- 88 mEq/L, HCO3- 34 mEq/L, and arterial pH 7.52. His urine pH is 5.5. What is the diagnosis, and how do you optimize him for the operating room?
  • Diagnosis: Hypertrophic Pyloric Stenosis presenting with hypokalemic, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis with paradoxical aciduria.
  • Action: **Do not schedule the patient for immediate surgery.** Pyloromyotomy is an elective procedure for a medical emergency; the patient must be biochemically optimized first.
  • Management Plan:
    • Establish IV access and initiate a fluid bolus of 20 mL/kg of 0.9% Normal Saline to restore intravascular volume.
    • Follow with maintenance and deficit replacement using D5W 0.45% NS with 20–30 mEq/L of Potassium Chloride.
    • **Target parameters for surgery:** Urine output > 1–2 mL/kg/hour, serum Cl- > 100 mEq/L, K+ > 3.5 mEq/L, and correction of paradoxical aciduria (urine pH becomes alkaline as potassium is replaced).
