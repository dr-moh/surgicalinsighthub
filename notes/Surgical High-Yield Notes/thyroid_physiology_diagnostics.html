Regulation of Thyroid Function & HPT Axis

1. Introduction & Hypothalamic-Pituitary Axis

The hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid (HPT) axis regulates systemic basal metabolic rate, thermogenesis, and cellular oxygen consumption through precise endocrine negative feedback loops. Hypothalamic thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) stimulates anterior pituitary thyrotropes to secrete thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH).

2. TSH Receptor Adenylate Cyclase Cascade

TSH binds to G-protein coupled TSH receptors (TSHR) on the basolateral membrane of thyroid follicular epithelial cells, activating adenylate cyclase to produce cyclic AMP (cAMP).

3. Sodium-Iodide Symporter (NIS) Mechanics

Basolateral iodide trapping occurs via the Sodium-Iodide Symporter (NIS), co-transporting 2 Na+ ions down their electrochemical gradient per 1 I- ion into the follicular cell.

4. Apical Transport and Pendrin Function

Iodide is transported across the apical follicular membrane into the colloid space via Pendrin (an anion exchanger mutated in Pendred syndrome).

5. Thyroid Peroxidase (TPO) Organification

On the apical membrane, Thyroid Peroxidase (TPO) catalyzes oxidation of iodide and organification onto tyrosine residues of thyroglobulin (Tg) scaffolds, forming monoiodotyrosine (MIT) and diiodotyrosine (DIT).

6. MIT and DIT Coupling Kinetics

TPO catalyzes coupling of MIT + DIT to form Triiodothyronine (T3), and DIT + DIT to form Thyroxine (T4), stored within colloid thyroglobulin colloid.

7. Peripheral Deiodination (D1, D2, D3)

CLINICAL PEARL — T3 VS T4 KINETICS The thyroid gland secretes predominantly Thyroxine (T4, ~85%) and a minor amount of Triiodothyronine (T3, ~15%). However, T3 is 4-times more potent metabolically than T4. Over 80% of circulating active T3 is generated in peripheral tissues via outer-ring 5'-deiodination catalyzed by Deiodinase type 1 (D1) and type 2 (D2). Inner-ring deiodination by D3 forms inactive reverse T3 (rT3).

8. Plasma Protein Binding & Free Hormone Fraction

Over 99.9% of circulating T4 is bound to plasma carrier proteins: Thyroxine-Binding Globulin (TBG, 70%), transthyretin (15%), and albumin (15%). Only the unbound free fraction is biologically active.

9. High-Yield Review & Board Exam Memory Anchors

  • Primary Active Transport: Basolateral Na+/I- Symporter (NIS).
  • Organification Enzyme: Apical Thyroid Peroxidase (TPO).
  • Most Active Hormone: T3 (generated peripherally via D1/D2).

1. Serum TSH 3rd Generation Immunoassays

Serum TSH measurement using 3rd generation immunometric assays (functional sensitivity 0.01 mIU/L) is the single most sensitive screening test for thyroid dysfunction. Due to log-linear feedback, a 2-fold change in Free T4 induces a 100-fold change in TSH.

2. Free T4 and Free T3 Direct Measurement

Free T4 (normal 0.9–1.7 ng/dL) measures unbound biologically active hormone. Direct analog immunoassays can be artifactually skewed by severe dysproteinemias or pregnancy.

3. Serum Thyroglobulin (Tg) Surveillance

Serum Thyroglobulin (Tg) serves as a surveillance tumor marker for recurrent differentiated thyroid carcinoma post-total thyroidectomy. Tg should drop to <1 ng/mL.

4. Anti-Thyroglobulin Antibody (TgAb) Interference

Concurrent measurement of TgAb is mandatory. Circulating anti-Tg antibodies falsely suppress immunometric Tg assays, producing false-negative cancer recurrence results.

5. Calcitonin and CEA Neuroendocrine Assays

Serum Calcitonin (>100 pg/mL diagnostic) and CEA serve as tumor markers for Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma surveillance.

6. Radioactive Iodine Uptake (RAIU) Scintigraphy

RAIU distinguishes hyperfunctioning synthesis (Graves, toxic adenoma = high diffuse or focal uptake) from follicular destruction (thyroiditis = low/absent uptake).

7. Fine-Needle Aspiration (FNA) Biopsy Techniques

Ultrasound-guided FNA using 22–25 gauge needles is the gold standard diagnostic evaluation for thyroid nodules ≥1 cm.

8. Molecular Testing Panels (ThyroSeq & Afirma)

Gene expression classifiers (Afirma GSC) and NGS mutation panels (ThyroSeq v3) risk-stratify indeterminate Bethesda III/IV nodules.

9. High-Yield Review & Diagnostic Assay Summary

Serum TSH 0.4 to 4.2 mIU/L First-line screening test. Log-linear sensitivity to free T4 changes.

1. Introduction to Autoimmune Thyroid Diseases (AITD)

Autoimmune thyroid diseases (AITD) result from immune tolerance loss, leading to autoantibodies targeting thyroid antigens: TPO, TSH receptor, and Thyroglobulin.

2. Anti-Thyroid Peroxidase Antibodies (TPOAb)

Anti-TPO antibodies target the enzyme responsible for organification. TPOAb is present in >95% of Hashimoto's thyroiditis and 50–70% of Graves' disease.

3. TSH Receptor Antibodies (TRAb & TSI)

TSH receptor autoantibodies are functionally divided into Thyroid-Stimulating Immunoglobulins (TSI, driving Graves' disease) and TSH-binding inhibitor immunoglobulins (TBII, blocking TSH and causing atrophic thyroiditis).

4. Anti-Thyroglobulin Antibodies (TgAb)

TgAb is present in 80% of Hashimoto's cases and serves as an essential co-test during thyroid cancer Tg surveillance.

5. Serologic Patterns in Hashimoto's Thyroiditis

Hashimoto's manifests marked elevation of TPOAb and TgAb, driving progressive cell-mediated follicular atrophy.

6. Serologic Patterns in Graves' Disease

Graves' disease is characterized by elevated stimulatory TSI autoantibodies. TPOAb is co-present in up to 70% of patients.

7. Transplacental Autoantibody Transfer

Maternal IgG TRAb/TSI crosses the placenta during the 3rd trimester, potentially triggering fetal or neonatal thyrotoxicosis.

8. Autoantibody Predictive Value in Subclinical Disease

In subclinical hypothyroidism (elevated TSH, normal FT4), positive TPOAb titers predict a 4.3% annual progression rate to overt hypothyroidism.

9. High-Yield Autoantibody Summary & Memory Anchors

  • Hashimoto Marker: Anti-TPO (>95%).
  • Graves Driver: TSI (TSH receptor stimulating antibody).
  • Immunoassay Confounder: Anti-Thyroglobulin (TgAb).

1. Introduction to High-Frequency Ultrasound

High-resolution real-time B-mode ultrasonography (using a 7.5–15 MHz linear transducer) is the essential imaging modality for evaluating thyroid parenchymal architecture, nodule stratification, and cervical lymph node basins.

2. Ultrasound Assessment of Parenchymal Disease

Graves' disease demonstrates a diffusely enlarged, hypoechoic gland with marked hypervascularity ("thyroid inferno" on color Doppler). Hashimoto's shows a coarse, pseudonodular, heterogeneous texture with fibrous septations.

3. Thyroid Nodule Sonographic Features

Nodule risk is determined by solid composition, hypoechogenicity, microlobulated or irregular margins, taller-than-wide shape, and microcalcifications.

4. Microcalcifications vs Macrocalcifications

Punctate hyperechoic foci without acoustic shadowing represent psammoma bodies (microcalcifications), highly specific for Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma.

5. Cervical Lymph Node Compartment Mapping

Normal lymph nodes are oval with a hyperechoic fatty hilum. Malignant nodal features include round shape, loss of fatty hilum, cystic change, microcalcifications, and peripheral hypervascularity.

6. Elastography and Doppler Flow Patterns

Real-time ultrasound elastography measures tissue hardness; hard, non-compressible nodules carry high malignancy risk. Intranodular hypervascularity correlates with neoplasia.

7. Ultrasound-Guided Interventional Procedures

Ultrasound guidance ensures precise needle tip placement during FNA of suspicious solid nodule components and suspicious cervical lymph nodes.

8. Preoperative Staging Ultrasonography Protocols

Routine preoperative ultrasound mapping of central (Level VI) and lateral (Levels II–V) neck compartments is mandatory for all biopsy-proven thyroid cancers.

9. High-Yield Review & Sonographic Board Anchors

BOARD WARNING — SUSPICIOUS ULTRASOUND FEATURES The ultrasound features most strongly predictive of thyroid malignancy are: microcalcifications (psammoma bodies), hypoechogenicity, irregular or microlobulated margins, a taller-than-wide shape (transverse view), and extrathyroidal extension.
