1. Introduction, Origin, and Cellular Lineages

Thyroid carcinoma encompasses a diverse spectrum of malignancies arising from two distinct parenchymal cell lineages within the thyroid gland: follicular epithelial cells (giving rise to Papillary, Follicular, Poorly Differentiated, and Anaplastic carcinomas) and neuroendocrine parafollicular C-cells (giving rise to Medullary thyroid carcinoma).

2. MAPK and PI3K-AKT Signaling Cascades

The mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway (RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK) is the primary oncologic driver in differentiated thyroid carcinoma. Activating point mutations in BRAF (specifically the BRAF V600E nucleotide substitution) and RAS (HRAS, KRAS, NRAS) or chromosomal rearrangements involving RET/PTC drive constituent cellular proliferation and anti-apoptosis.

3. BRAF V600E and RAS Mutations

Molecular research establishes two mutually exclusive driver paradigms: BRAF-like and RAS-like signaling. BRAF V600E-mutated tumors exhibit high MAPK activity, severe downregulation of thyroid metabolizing genes (NIS, TPO), and aggressive lymph node metastasis.

4. Chromosomal Rearrangements (RET/PTC & PAX8-PPARγ)

Chromosomal translocations generate chimeric oncogenes. RET/PTC1 and RET/PTC3 rearrangements are strongly associated with childhood post-radiation papillary carcinomas, whereas PAX8-PPARγ fusions characterize follicular neoplasms.

5. TERT Promoter Mutations & Co-Mutational Synergy

BOARD WARNING — BRAF V600E & TERT PROMOTER CO-MUTATION The combination of a BRAF V600E point mutation co-existing with a TERT promoter mutation (C228T or C250T) represents an exceptionally high-risk molecular signature associated with aggressive extrathyroidal extension, massive nodal metastases, radioiodine refractoriness, and high disease-specific mortality.

6. Anaplastic Dedifferentiation Dynamics

Anaplastic transformation occurs when well-differentiated tumors accumulate late-stage inactivating mutations in TP53 (>70% of ATC) and activating mutations in PIK3CA or β-catenin.

7. Differentiating Biomarkers & Gene Expression

Loss of thyroid differentiation markers correlates with therapeutic RAI resistance. BRAF-mutated tumors demonstrate marked suppression of the sodium-iodide symporter (NIS).

8. Targeted Molecular Diagnostics

Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) molecular panels (ThyroSeq v3, Afirma GSC) guide surgical decision-making for cytologically indeterminate thyroid nodules.

9. High-Yield Review & Examination Concepts

  • Papillary Carcinoma Driver: BRAF V600E (40–60%), RET/PTC.
  • Follicular Carcinoma Driver: RAS mutations, PAX8-PPARγ fusion.
  • Lethal Synergy: BRAF V600E + TERT promoter mutation.

1. Introduction, Epidemiology, and Classification

Differentiated thyroid carcinoma (DTC) accounts for >90% of all thyroid malignancies. Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most prevalent variant (85%), followed by follicular thyroid carcinoma (FTC) (10%). Overall 10-year survival exceeds 95% for early-stage disease.

2. Histopathology and Classical Nuclear Features

Pathologically, PTC is defined by distinct nuclear features: ground-glass "Orphan Annie eye" nuclei, nuclear psammoma bodies, intranuclear cytoplasmic inclusions, and nuclear grooves.

3. Aggressive Papillary Histologic Variants

  • Tall Cell Variant: Height-to-width ratio ≥3 in ≥30% of cells. Associated with vascular invasion and RAI resistance.
  • Hobnail & Diffuse Sclerosing Variants: Aggressive subtypes with microcalcifications and early distant pulmonary metastases.

4. Follicular Carcinoma Diagnostic Criteria

CLINICAL PEARL — FOLLICULAR CARCINOMA DIAGNOSIS Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) cannot differentiate benign Follicular Adenoma from malignant Follicular Carcinoma. Diagnosis of FTC strictly requires demonstration of capsular invasion or vascular invasion on permanent histology after diagnostic lobectomy.

5. Clinical Staging and AJCC 8th Edition Framework

AJCC 8th Edition utilizes an age cutoff of 55 years. Patients <55 years old are Stage I (any T, any N, M0) or Stage II (M1). Patients ≥55 years are stratified by T and N stage.

6. Radioactive Iodine (RAI) Remnant Ablation

Postoperative I-131 ablation is administered for intermediate-risk (vascular invasion, LN metastases) and high-risk (extrathyroidal extension) patients following thyroid hormone withdrawal or recombinant human TSH (rhTSH) stimulation.

7. TSH Suppression Therapy Protocols

Levothyroxine suppresses TSH to minimize stimulation of residual thyroid cells. High-risk patients target TSH <0.1 mIU/L; low-risk patients target 0.5–2.0 mIU/L.

8. Surveillance Biomarkers (Tg & TgAb)

Serum Thyroglobulin (Tg) serves as a sensitive recurrence marker. Concurrent measurement of anti-Thyroglobulin antibodies (TgAb) is mandatory to prevent false-negative immunoassay results.

9. Examination Revision and High-Yield Summary

  • PTC Diagnostic Feature: Psammoma bodies & Orphan Annie eye nuclei.
  • FTC Malignancy Proof: Capsular/vascular invasion on lobectomy histology.
  • AJCC Age Threshold: 55 years old.

1. Introduction, Origins & Epidemiology

Medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) is a distinct neuroendocrine malignant neoplasm originating from the parafollicular or calcitonin-producing C cells of the thyroid gland (Schmid 2015). In contrast to thyroid follicular epithelial cells, C cells are of neuroendocrine origin; they do not respond to thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), do not synthesize thyroglobulin (Tg), and lack the active transport mechanisms required to trap radioiodine. Traditionally, mammalian C cells were believed to derive exclusively from neural crest-derived mesenchyme migrating into the lateral thyroid lobes via the ultimobranchial body. However, recent developmental biology studies (Johansson et al. 2015, Nilsson & Williams 2016) have put forward an endodermal origin for C cell progenitors, strongly supported by constituent cellular expression of E-cadherin (Kameda et al. 2007).

Epidemiologically, MTC accounts for 5–10% of all clinical thyroid malignancies, 0.4–1.4% of all evaluated thyroid nodules, and approximately 0.14% in unselected autopsy series (Valle & Kloos 2011). Population-based SEER registry analyses demonstrate that the incidence of MTC increased from 0.14 to 0.21 per 100,000 population between 1983 and 2012 in the United States, regardless of tumor stage at diagnosis (Randle et al. 2017). Unlike papillary (PTC) and follicular (FTC) thyroid carcinomas, MTC displays no gender predominance (1:1 female-to-male ratio). The peak clinical incidence occurs in the 4th and 5th decades of life, with a minor increase in mean age at diagnosis from 50 to 54 years over the last 30 years (Randle et al. 2017). Pediatric presentation is extremely rare and almost invariably signifies an inherited familial/hereditary germline syndrome (Pelizzo et al. 2007). Environmental or ethnic risk factors for MTC development remain unknown.

MTC is classified into two major clinical forms: Sporadic MTC (75%), presenting as unifocal disease, and Hereditary/Familial MTC (25%), presenting as an autosomal dominant inherited syndrome with variable expressivity and age-related penetrance. Historical series report a 10-year overall survival of approximately 50%. However, modern multidisciplinary referral management has significantly improved 5-year disease-specific survival from 86% to 89% overall, with marked improvements in patients presenting with regional nodal disease (from 82% to 91%) and distant metastatic disease (from 40% to 51%), driven by extensive primary surgical compartment clearance including thyroidectomy and lymphadenectomy (Randle et al. 2017).

2. Molecular Pathogenesis & RET/RAS Oncogene Mechanics

The molecular pathogenesis of MTC is defined by genetic alterations in receptor tyrosine kinase signaling pathways. Germline or somatic activating point mutations in the RET proto-oncogene drive virtually 100% of hereditary MTC cases and 40–80% of sporadic cases (Romei et al. 2016b). Somatic RAS point mutations represent the second most common genetic alteration in sporadic MTC (Ciampi et al. 2013).

The RET proto-oncogene is a 21-exon gene located on chromosome 10q11.2 encoding a single-pass transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase (TK) expressed in neural crest-derived cell lineages, including thyroid C cells and the adrenal medulla. Physiological activation requires binding of glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) family ligands (GDNF, neurturin, artemin, persephin) in complex with GFRα co-receptors. Oncogenic point mutations induce ligand-independent dimerization and autophosphorylation of intracellular tyrosine residues, triggering constitutive activation of downstream pro-survival, proliferative, angiogenic, and migratory pathways: the MAPK pathway (RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK) and the PI3K-AKT pathway (Romei et al. 2016b).

3. Hereditary Syndromes & ATA Risk Stratification

In 1993, independent groups identified activating germline point mutations in RET as the causative event in MEN 2A and FMTC (Donis-Keller et al. 1993, Mulligan et al. 1993), followed by MEN 2B in 1994 (Eng et al. 1994). Approximately 98% of MEN 2A cases involve RET mutations in the extracellular cysteine-rich domain, specifically codons 609, 611, 618, 620 in exon 10 and codon 634 in exon 11. Germline mutations at codon 634 (specifically C634R) account for 85% of MEN 2A cases and strongly correlate with the co-development of Pheochromocytoma, Parathyroid Adenomas, and Cutaneous Lichen Amyloidosis (Eng et al. 1996, Raue & Frank-Raue 2009).

MEN 2B is almost invariably driven by a specific intracellular catalytic tyrosine kinase domain mutation in exon 16: M918T (>95% of cases), conferring aggressive infantile oncogenicity. Rare MEN 2B cases harbor exon 15 A883F mutations (Mathiesen et al. 2017) or double mutations at codons 804/904 (Menko et al. 2002). FMTC involves mutations distributed across cysteine codons or non-cysteine codons (exon 13 E768D, L790F; exon 14 V804L/M; exon 15 S891A), demonstrating lower aggressiveness and older age at diagnosis (Raue & Frank-Raue 2009). In 4–10% of MEN 2A/FMTC and ~95% of MEN 2B patients, the mutation is a de novo alteration arising on the paternal allele (Schuffenecker et al. 1997). Rare Variants of Unknown Significance (VUS) require careful surveillance rather than immediate surgery (Cosci et al. 2011, Lebeault et al. 2017).

4. Clinical Presentation & Syndromic Manifestations

Sporadic MTC presents clinically as a solitary thyroid nodule or dominant nodule in a multinodular goiter. Patients are usually euthyroid and asymptomatic. In advanced stage IV disease with heavy tumor burden and high serum calcitonin, tumor co-secretion of calcitonin, vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP), serotonin, and prostaglandins causes severe secretory diarrhea and cutaneous facial flushing (Alam 1994, Hannah-Shmouni et al. 2016).

Familial MTC presents as bilateral, multicentric nodular disease associated with C-cell hyperplasia (Schmid 2015). In MEN 2A, hyperparathyroidism occurs in ~30% (mean age 3rd–4th decades) as asymptomatic hypercalcemia or multiple adenomatosis/hyperplasia (Alevizaki & Saltiki 2015). Pheochromocytoma develops in 50% of MEN 2A and 40–45% of MEN 2B patients (Mucha et al. 2017); unlike sporadic pheochromocytoma, MEN 2 adrenal tumors are characteristically bilateral and multicentric (often asynchronously developed over a 10-year window). MEN 2B manifests pathognomonic physical signs: marfanoid habitus (long limbs relative to trunk, pectus excavatum), skeletal deformities (scoliosis, lordosis, polydactyly; Brauckhoff et al. 2004), thickened lips with prominent mucosal neuromas on the tongue, buccal mucosa, and conjunctiva, and infantile intestinal ganglioneuromatosis causing megacolon and severe motility cramping (Erdogan et al. 2006). Cutaneous Lichen Amyloidosis (CLA)—an intensely pruritic, hyperpigmented papular lesion in the interscapular back region—occurs in ~10% of MEN 2A kindreds and is pathognomonic when present (Ceccherini et al. 1994).

5. Diagnostic Workup, FNAC Limitations & Calcitonin Dynamics

Presurgical evaluation utilizes neck physical examination, ultrasonography, fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC), and biochemical tumor markers:

  • Ultrasonography: MTC typically displays a hypoechoic nodule with acoustic shadowing microcalcifications. However, sonographic patterns are non-specific and mimic other thyroid cancer histotypes (Lee et al. 2010).
  • FNAC Diagnostic Limitations: Presurgical FNA cytology fails to correctly identify MTC in >50% of cases in large international multicenter series (313 patients across 12 referral centers; Essig et al. 2013), leading to inadequate initial surgical resection in >60% of patients. Diagnostic failure stems from variable cellular morphology (plasmacytoid, spindle, or giant cell patterns) mimicking follicular neoplasms, Hurthle cell tumors, or anaplastic cancer (Pacini et al. 1994, Rieu et al. 1995, Niccoli et al. 1997, Forrest et al. 1998). FNAC accuracy is dramatically improved by performing calcitonin immunocytochemistry on cytological smears or measuring calcitonin in needle washout fluid (Boi et al. 2007).
  • Serum Calcitonin (Ct): Calcitonin (a 32-amino acid polypeptide produced by C cells; Melvin & Tashjian 1968) is the most sensitive and specific biomarker for MTC. Basal serum Ct >100 pg/mL is 100% diagnostic of MTC (Costante et al. 2007). For moderately elevated levels (20–100 pg/mL), a Calcium Stimulation Test (25 mg/kg calcium gluconate IV over 5 min) differentiates MTC from non-thyroidal causes. Routine serum Ct screening in thyroid nodular disease detects early stage I unsuspected sporadic MTC, significantly improving survival (Elisei et al. 2004a, Cheung et al. 2008, Wells et al. 2015).
  • Serum Carcinoembryonic Antigen (CEA): Co-secreted by MTC cells. Elevated CEA correlates with bulky disease and distant metastases (Rougier et al. 1983). Serial CEA monitoring tracks disease progression; rising CEA alongside falling calcitonin indicates aggressive tumor dedifferentiation. Other secretory products include Chromogranin-A (Baudin et al. 2001), Procalcitonin (Machens et al. 2014), Somatostatin, CGRP, VIP, and NSE (Pacini et al. 1991, Barakat et al. 2004).
  • Germline RET Genetic Screening: Mandatory in ALL patients with documented MTC (sporadic and familial) to identify unsuspected hereditary germline transmission (found in 5–7% of apparently sporadic cases; Romei et al. 2011, Elisei et al. 2013a) and enable screening of first-degree relatives.

6. Histopathology, Amyloid & C-Cell Hyperplasia Criteria

Macroscopically, MTC presents as a hard, firm, unencapsulated chalky-white or reddish tumor mass (Schmid 2015). Histologically, malignant C cells exhibit spindle-shaped or rounded polygonal morphology arranged in nests, cords, or insular patterns separated by fibrovascular stroma. Nuclei display uniform "salt-and-pepper" chromatin, and granular eosinophilic cytoplasm contains calcitonin secretory vesicles.

A pathognomonic histologic hallmark present in 60–80% of MTC specimens is stromal amyloid deposition composed of misfolded pro-calcitonin fibrils (Sletten et al. 1976). Amyloid stains positive with Congo Red, displaying classic apple-green birefringence under polarized light microscopy. Immunohistochemistry is strongly positive for Calcitonin, CEA, Chromogranin-A, Synaptophysin, and TTF-1, while remaining strictly negative for Thyroglobulin (Tg) (Giovanella et al. 2008, Trimboli & Giovanella 2015).

C-Cell Hyperplasia (CCH)—defined as >50 C-cells per single low-power field (100x) or anatomical filling of follicles (LiVolsi 1997)—is the established pre-neoplastic precursor lesion present in virtually 100% of hereditary MTC cases and ~30% of sporadic cases (Nikiforov et al. 2012). Mixed collision tumors containing concurrent separate foci of MTC and Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma (PTC) occur in clinical practice, driven by distinct molecular alterations rather than single stem cell divergence (Matias-Guiu 1999, Biscolla et al. 2004, Wong et al. 2012, Ciampi et al. 2017).

7. Post-Treatment Surveillance & Doubling Time Kinetics

Postoperative surveillance evaluates surgical cure completeness. Postoperative serum basal and stimulated calcitonin should be measured 3 months after surgery to allow complete metabolic degradation of preoperative circulating protein pools (Fugazzola et al. 1994):

  • Undetectable Calcitonin (<2–5 pg/mL): Confirms complete biochemical cure, associated with an estimated long-term recurrence risk of 10% (Pellegriti et al. 2003), which drops to 3.3% if post-op stimulation testing is negative (Franc et al. 2001). Surveillance requires annual Ct and neck US.
  • Detectable Calcitonin / Biochemical Disease: Approximately 50% of patients not cured by primary surgery have no visible anatomical tumor on traditional imaging ("biochemical disease"). When serum Ct is <150 pg/mL, anatomical localization is rarely achieved, and guidelines recommend a conservative "wait and see" management strategy (Meijer et al. 2010, Schlumberger et al. 2012a).
  • Calcitonin & CEA Doubling Time (DT): The single most powerful prognostic biomarker for disease progression and mortality (Gawlik et al. 2010). Calculate DT using serial measurements over 12–24 months:
    • DT < 6 months (or <1 year): Indicates rapid progressive metastatic recurrence and high mortality rate (Meijer et al. 2010). Triggers immediate systemic imaging staging.
    • DT > 2 years: Indicates indolent stable disease with excellent long-term survival.
  • Advanced Localization Imaging: For progressive disease (Ct >150 pg/mL or rapid DT), imaging includes neck US, contrast CT neck/chest/abdomen, liver MRI, 18F-DOPA PET/CT (the most sensitive PET radiotracer for MTC; Skoura 2013, Giraudet & Taieb 2017), or 68Ga-DOTATATE / DOTATOC PET/CT (Baudin et al. 1996, Giraudet et al. 2007). Selective venous sampling is obsolete.

8. Primary Surgical Resection & Prophylactic Guidelines

Because C cells do not concentrate radioiodine (RAI) and TSH suppression has zero therapeutic efficacy, surgery is the ONLY curative treatment for MTC. Early complete surgical clearance dictates long-term survival (Pelizzo et al. 2007, Randle et al. 2017, Raue et al. 2018):

MANDATORY SURGICAL BOARD RULE — PHEOCHROMOCYTOMA RULE-OUT Prior to undergoing neck surgery, ALL patients with MTC or RET mutations MUST be screened for Pheochromocytoma via plasma free metanephrines or 24-hour urinary fractionated metanephrines (Wells et al. 2013, 2015). Operating on an unrecognized, unblocked pheochromocytoma can precipitate fatal intraoperative hypertensive crisis. If a pheochromocytoma is identified, laparoscopic adrenalectomy MUST precede thyroidectomy.

Standard initial surgical procedures comprise:

  1. Total Thyroidectomy: Mandatory for all MTC cases due to high rates of multicentricity and bilaterality (100% in hereditary, 20–30% in sporadic; Essig et al. 2016). Subtotal thyroidectomy is obsolete.
  2. Central Neck Lymph Node Dissection (Level VI): Routine bilateral Level VI dissection (prelaryngeal, pretracheal, paratracheal nodes) is performed in ALL clinical MTC cases because 50–60% of patients harbor occult central node metastases at initial presentation (Moley & DeBenedetti 1999, Ukkat et al. 2004). Correlation with preop Ct: basal Ct <20 pg/mL carries zero central node risk; Ct <50 pg/mL carries <10% risk (Machens & Dralle 2010). ATA Guidelines (Wells et al. 2015): RET carriers with serum Ct <40 pg/mL may undergo total thyroidectomy alone (Rohmer et al. 2011, Elisei et al. 2012).
  3. Lateral Neck Lymph Node Dissection (Levels II–V): "En bloc" modified radical lateral neck dissection is indicated when preop US confirms lateral nodal metastases, or when primary tumor size is >2 cm or basal serum Ct is >200 pg/mL (lateral nodal metastasis risk >75-90%; Scollo et al. 2003, Machens & Dralle 2010).

9. Advanced Disease, Targeted Biologics & High-Yield Review

For unresectable, progressive, or symptomatic systemic metastatic MTC (liver, lung, bone metastases), traditional cytotoxic chemotherapy yields poor response rates. Modern systemic therapy utilizes oral targeted small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs):

  • Highly Selective RET Inhibitors (First-Line Standard): Selpercatinib (LOXO-292) and Pralsetinib (BLU-667) are highly selective RET kinase inhibitors demonstrating objective response rates (ORR) >60–70% in RET-mutated MTC with durable progression-free survival and favorable safety profiles.
  • Multi-Kinase Inhibitors (FDA Approved): Vandetanib (ZACTIMA) and Cabozantinib (COMETRIQ) inhibit RET, VEGFR2/3, and EGFR, significantly prolonging progression-free survival in advanced progressive MTC (Wells et al. 2012, Elisei et al. 2013b, Schlumberger et al. 2012b).
  • Palliative Management of Secretory Symptoms: Severe secretory diarrhea and cutaneous flushing are palliated using long-acting Somatostatin Analogs (Octreotide LAR, Lanreotide) or Telotristat ethyl. Painful bone metastases or airway compression are managed with external beam radiation therapy (EBRT), chemoembolization, or radiofrequency ablation.

1. Introduction, Epidemiology, and Classification

Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC) is one of the most lethal human malignancies, accounting for <2% of thyroid cancers but >50% of thyroid cancer-related mortality. Median overall survival is 4 to 6 months. All ATC tumors are classified as Stage IV (IVA localized to thyroid, IVB extrathyroidal extension, IVC distant metastases).

2. Molecular Pathogenesis & TP53 Driver Mutations

ATC develops via dedifferentiation of preexisting differentiated thyroid carcinomas. Tumors accumulate late-stage inactivating mutations in TP53 (>70% of ATC) and activating mutations in PIK3CA or β-catenin superimposed on precursor BRAF or RAS mutations.

3. Clinical Presentation and Rapid Compressive Symptoms

ATC presents as a rapidly expanding, rock-hard anterior neck mass causing acute compressive symptoms: dysphagia, stridor, dyspnea, and recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy.

4. Diagnostic Evaluation & Rapid NGS Sequencing

Core needle or open incisional biopsy is performed immediately. Biopsy tissue must undergo urgent Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) or immunohistochemistry for BRAF V600E status within 48–72 hours.

5. Airway Management & Emergency Resuscitation

Airway preservation is the primary clinical goal. Tracheostomy is avoided if possible due to high rates of tumor fungation and stomal recurrence, but is performed for impending asphyxia.

6. Surgical Resection Indications

Surgical resection (total thyroidectomy with neck dissection) is indicated only for localized Stage IVA disease where gross R0/R1 resection can be achieved without sacrificing major neurovascular structures.

7. Multi-Modal Systemic & Radiation Protocols

Unresectable BRAF-wildtype tumors undergo hyperfractionated external beam radiation therapy (EBRT) combined with radiosensitizing cytotoxic chemotherapy (Paclitaxel or Cisplatin).

8. Targeted Biologics (Dabrafenib + Trametinib)

Patients harboring BRAF V600E mutations receive targeted therapy with Dabrafenib (BRAF inhibitor) + Trametinib (MEK inhibitor). This combination achieves rapid tumor regression, enabling secondary surgical resection in previously unresectable patients.

9. High-Yield Board Summary & Prognostic Review

  • ATC Staging: All cases are Stage IV (IVA, IVB, IVC).
  • Key Molecular Alteration: TP53 mutation (>70%).
  • Targeted Biologic Pair: Dabrafenib + Trametinib for BRAF V600E.

2. Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve (RLN) Dissection Mechanics

The RLN is identified within Simon's triangle (bounded by common carotid laterally, trachea medially, inferior thyroid artery superiorly). The nerve passes under the Zuckerkandl tubercle and enters the larynx posterior to Berry's ligament (suspensory ligament of the thyroid). Ligate Berry's ligament with extreme care to avoid traction or thermal nerve injury.

3. Superior Laryngeal Nerve (EBSLN) Protection

The External Branch of the Superior Laryngeal Nerve (EBSLN) innervates the cricothyroid muscle. It travels in Joll's space closely adherent to the superior thyroid artery. To prevent nerve injury (causing loss of high-pitch voice), superior pole vessels must be ligated individually right on the capsule of the thyroid lobe.

4. Parathyroid Preservation and Autotransplantation

Preserve parathyroid vascularity from the inferior thyroid artery. If a parathyroid gland is devascularized or accidentally resected, verify its histology by frozen section/water flotation and autotransplant it into biopsy-proven pockets within the ipsilateral sternocleidomastoid or brachioradialis muscle.

5. Level VI Central Compartment Dissection

Level VI is bounded by the hyoid bone superiorly, innominate artery inferiorly, and carotid sheaths laterally. It contains prelaryngeal (Delphian), pretracheal, and paratracheal lymph nodes. Central dissection is mandatory in MTC and therapeutic in cN1a DTC.

6. Levels II-V Lateral Neck Dissection Boundaries

Level VI (Central Compartment) Hyoid bone superiorly, innominate artery inferiorly, carotid sheaths laterally. Mandatory in MTC and cN1a DTC; recommended in T3/T4 DTC.
Levels II–V (Lateral Compartment) Level II (upper jugular), Level III (middle jugular), Level IV (lower jugular), Level V (posterior triangle). Therapeutic lateral neck dissection performed when biopsy-proven lateral nodal metastasis is present (cN1b).

7. Systemic Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor (TKI) Therapies

Patients with radioiodine-refractory progressive DTC undergo systemic therapy with multi-kinase inhibitors Lenvatinib (SELECT trial) or Sorafenib (DECISION trial), which significantly prolong progression-free survival.

8. Postoperative Complications & Emergency Protocols

POSTOPERATIVE EMERGENCY — COMPRESSIVE HEMATOMA MANAGEMENT Post-thyroidectomy neck hematoma is a life-threatening airway emergency causing venous congestion and laryngeal edema. If a patient develops stridor, respiratory distress, or expanding neck swelling, DO NOT wait for an OR or transport. Immediately open the surgical wound (cut skin sutures and open strap muscles) at the bedside to evacuate the clot and relieve airway compression before transferring to the OR for formal hemostasis.

9. Examination Revision and Surgical Board Anchors

  • RLN Crossing: Posterior to inferior thyroid artery.
  • EBSLN Protection: Ligate superior pole vessels individually on thyroid capsule.
  • Compressive Hematoma Action: Immediate bedside wound decompression.
